## 2. G1: Amina's metre bridge (flawed). Copy exactly

**Top of the page (for the privacy-blur test):**
> NAME: Amina Yusuf (a made-up name)   SUBJECT: Physics Practical   DATE: 23/10/2026

**Heading:** METRE BRIDGE

**The question (as a student would copy it):**
> You are provided with a metre bridge AC, a resistance box R, a resistor X, a Leclanché cell E, a centre-zero galvanometer G, a key K, a jockey J and connecting wires. Find the balance length l for R = 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 Ω. Plot l⁻¹ against R⁻¹ and evaluate X = s/c.

**The diagram** (top half of the page, about 8 cm tall), labelled with arrows:
- the metre bridge wire AC drawn as a long straight line with "0 cm" at A and "100 cm" at C
- the resistance box R in the left gap and X in the right gap, meeting at B
- the galvanometer G (a circle with G) from B down to the jockey J touching the wire
- the cell E across A and C
- **deliberately leave out the key K** (the examiner should circle this)

**The results table**, with ruled lines. Write the headers exactly like this (note the missing unit on the last one):

| R (Ω) | l (cm) | R⁻¹ (Ω⁻¹) | l⁻¹ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 25.3 | 1.00 | 0.0395 |
| 2.0 | 39.8 | 0.500 | 0.0251 |
| 3.0 | 50.2 | 0.333 | **0.02** |
| 4.0 | **57** | 0.250 | 0.0175 |
| 5.0 | 62.4 | 0.200 | 0.0160 |

**The graph** (on the graph sheet, in pencil): draw it **small, about a quarter of the sheet** (about 8 cm wide and 6 cm tall).
- Axes from the origin. Horizontal axis labelled "R⁻¹ (Ω⁻¹)" from 0 to 1.0. Vertical axis labelled only **"l⁻¹"** (no unit) from 0 to 0.04.
- Plot the five points from her table (plot the third at 0.020, as she wrote it). Draw a ruled line of best fit through them, continued back to the vertical axis.
- Draw a **small** slope triangle between the two neighbouring points at R⁻¹ = 0.200 and 0.250.
- Beside it write: "s = (0.0175 − 0.0160) ÷ (0.250 − 0.200) = 0.030", "c = 0.010", "X = s/c = 0.030 ÷ 0.010 = 3.0 Ω".

**Precautions** (under the table, word for word):
> 1. Avoid parallax error when reading the metre rule.
> 2. I slid the jockey gently along the wire to find the balance point.

**The mistakes the examiner must catch (the answer key; scheme in doc 15 §4):**
| Mistake | Rule | Marks lost |
|---|---|---|
| "57" not to 1 d.p. | PHYS-1DP | ½ |
| "0.02" not to 3 s.f. | PHYS-3SF | ½ |
| No unit on the l⁻¹ column | PHYS-TABLE | ½ |
| No unit on the vertical axis | GRAPH-AXES | ½ |
| Graph about ¼ of the sheet (needs at least ⅓) | GRAPH-SCALE | ½ |
| Small slope triangle between neighbouring points | GRAPH-SLOPE | 1 |
| "Avoid parallax error…" is an instruction, not stated in acceptable (past) tense | PRECAUTIONS | 1 |
| "I slid the jockey…" is the mistake WAEC penalises, not a precaution | PRECAUTIONS | 1 |
| No key in the circuit diagram | Setup issue (circled, no mark) | — |

Expected result: **15½/21** in student mode. Her X = 3.0 Ω is evaluated correctly from her own s and c, so PHYS-EVAL is earned. The true X is 3.0 Ω, and the app never shows that to students.

## 3. G2: Amina's metre bridge (clean)

Same page and question, but:
- The **key K is drawn** in series with the cell.
- Table headers: "R (Ω) | l (cm) | R⁻¹ (Ω⁻¹) | l⁻¹ (cm⁻¹)". The 4th row reads **57.0** and the 3rd row's l⁻¹ reads **0.0199**.
- The graph fills **at least half** of the sheet. Vertical axis "l⁻¹ (cm⁻¹)", horizontal axis "R⁻¹ (Ω⁻¹)", both from the origin, with scales of 2, 5 or 10 per large square (never 3).
- A **large** slope triangle from R⁻¹ = 0.15 to 1.00 on the line: "s = (0.0396 − 0.0146) ÷ (1.00 − 0.15) = 0.0294 Ω cm⁻¹", "c = 0.0102 cm⁻¹", "X = s/c = 2.88 Ω".
- Precautions: "1. The key was opened between readings. 2. The jockey was not dragged along the wire."

Expected result: **21/21** (and 22/22 in Teacher mode with a teacher value of 3.0 Ω, since 2.88 is within ±10%).

## 4. G3: Amina's titration (flawed). Copy exactly

**Top of the page (for the privacy-blur test):**
> NAME: Amina Yusuf (write a made-up name)   SUBJECT: Chemistry Practical   DATE: 22/10/2026

**Heading:** ACID–BASE TITRATION

**The question (write it as a student would copy it):**
> A is 0.100 mol dm⁻³ HCl. B is a solution of sodium trioxocarbonate(IV), Na₂CO₃. Put A into the burette and titrate it against 25.0 cm³ portions of B using methyl orange as indicator.

**The diagram** (left half of the page, about 10 cm tall), clearly labelled with arrows:
- retort stand and clamp
- burette (with a few graduation marks and the label "A (HCl)")
- tap / stopcock
- conical flask (with liquid drawn inside, label "B + methyl orange")
- white tile under the flask

Leave **the funnel off** in G3 and G4 (in G3 you can optionally draw a funnel still in the burette as an extra "setup mistake" for the AI to notice).

**The results table** (right half), with ruled lines:

| Burette reading | Rough | 1st | 2nd | 3rd |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Final reading (cm³) | 25.10 | 24.60 | **24.5** | 24.70 |
| Initial reading (cm³) | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.10 |
| Volume of acid used (cm³) | 25.10 | 24.60 | **24.5** | 24.60 |

**Below the table, write the deliberate mistakes:**
> Average titre = (25.10 + 24.60 + 24.5) ÷ 3 = 24.73
> (note: **no unit**, and it **includes the rough titre** and leaves out the 3rd titre)

**Equation (deliberately missing state symbols):**
> 2HCl + Na₂CO₃ → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂

**End point:** "colour changed from yellow to orange"

**The mistakes the examiner must catch (this is the answer key):**
| Mistake | Rule |
|---|---|
| "24.5" in the 2nd column (twice) | CHEM-BUR-2DP: burette readings to 2 d.p. (24.50) |
| Rough titre included in the average | CHEM-ROUGH / CHEM-CONCORD: average only concordant titres |
| 3rd titre (24.60) left out although it is concordant | CHEM-CONCORD |
| No unit on the average titre | CHEM-3SF / units |
| No state symbols in the equation | CHEM-3SF (state symbols) |
| (optional) funnel left in the burette | Setup issue |

The correct average from concordant titres is (24.60 + 24.50 + 24.60) ÷ 3 = **24.57 cm³**, which gives the concentration of B as 0.100 × 24.57 ÷ (2 × 25.0) = **0.0491 mol dm⁻³**. Never show this to students in the app; it is for testing only.

## 5. G4: Amina's titration (clean, optional)

Same page layout and question, but:
- Table: 2nd column reads **24.50** (both rows).
- Average: "Average titre = (24.60 + 24.50 + 24.60) ÷ 3 = **24.57 cm³**", with a line "1st, 2nd and 3rd titres are concordant".

## 6. G5: Tunde's glass block (stretch only; pencil, drawing paper)

Use a real rectangular glass block if you can borrow one (typical size about 11 × 6.5 cm). If not, draw a 11.0 cm × 6.5 cm rectangle with a ruler.

**Five traces**, each with **its own outline** of the block (WAEC deducts if all traces share one outline). For each trace:
- Draw the outline, a normal at the point of incidence on the long face, and the incident ray at angle i.
- Mark pin positions P1, P2 on the incident ray (about 5 cm apart) and P3, P4 on the emergent side, as small dots or circles.
- Join to show the refracted ray inside the block and the emergent ray.
- Label i and r.

Use these angles and approximate values (for n ≈ 1.5, block 6.5 cm thick):

| Trace | i (°) | r (°) | d (cm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 | 6.6 | 0.4 |
| 2 | 20 | 13.2 | 0.8 |
| 3 | 30 | 19.5 | 1.3 |
| 4 | 40 | 25.4 | 1.8 |
| 5 | 50 | 30.7 | 2.5 |

Write the readings table on the same paper (lengths to 1 d.p.).

**Deliberate mistakes in G5:**
- **No arrows on any trace** (−½, once).
- **Trace 4 has no normal drawn.**
Everything else is correct.

## 7. M1–M3: real messy pages
Ask 2–3 students or recent leavers (18+ is simplest, or with consent) for **photos of real pages** from their practical notebooks: titration tables and glass-block traces as they actually wrote them. Don't fix anything. These show judges it works on real work, not just your neat pages.

## 8. Photographing (the golden images and the 30+ test set)

**Golden images (G1, G2, G3; G5 if drawn):** 3 lighting conditions each:
1. **Window light**, phone held flat above the page (the hero image).
2. **Indoor bulb**, slightly warm, a little shadow.
3. **Evening / fluorescent**, slightly dim.
Shoot each straight down **and** at a slight angle (about 20°) so the corner finder has something to correct.

**Test set (30+ photos):**
| Group | Count | Variety |
|---|---|---|
| Metre bridge pages with graphs (G1, G2, M-pages, other students) | 12+ | Different biros, pencil graphs, neat and messy |

## 9. Audio set (10–20 clips, consenting adults)

Record short answers (5–20 s each) on your phone in a normal room. Mix English, Pidgin and code-switched. Suggested prompts (from the examiner question bank):
1. "Why must you not slide the jockey along the wire?"
2. "Why is the bridge wire made of constantan?"
3. "Why do you open the key between readings?"
4. "Why do you use methyl orange and not phenolphthalein for a carbonate?"
5. "What makes two titres concordant?"
6. "Why do you remove the funnel before reading the burette?"
7. "Which part of the meniscus do you read?"

For each clip, write down **exactly what was said** (the true transcript). Name files `audio_q1_pidgin_speakerA.m4a` etc. Save in `SnapLabNG/dataset/audio/`.
