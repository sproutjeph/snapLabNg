# SnapLab NG: 3-minute demo voiceover

Record on https://snaplab-ng-2026.web.app at laptop size, sound on. Speak slowly and warmly; ~150 words a minute.
**[Screen]** is what to show, **VO** is what you say. Where the app speaks (the welcome, the question being read), stay
quiet and let it play. Before you record: open the site once (warm cache), do one Practise run to hand-in, and set the
browser zoom so the whole bench fits.

---

**0:00–0:15 · Hook**
[Screen] Home page. The welcome voice plays; the 3D bench turns on the Ohm's law card.
VO: "Last year nearly two million Nigerian students sat the WASSCE. For physics, they must pass a practical exam,
and many of their schools have no lab. They learn experiments they have never touched. This is SnapLab."

**0:15–0:50 · Snap your question (Meta reads the paper)**
[Screen] Tap **Snap your question** → **Try a sample** → **Read my question**. Open **Show the AI**.
VO: "A student photographs her practical question and its diagram. Meta's Muse Spark reads it word for word, works
out which practical it is, and finds every part drawn in the diagram. SAM 3.1 then outlines each part on her photo.
Here: Ohm's law, WASSCE 2018, question 3B, with the resistance box, ammeter, key, cells, wire and voltmeter, all found
in a single read. Every model call, and how long it took, is right here."

**0:50–1:40 · Watch (the question drives the bench)**
[Screen] **Build the bench** → **Watch it done**. Let the app read; parts drop as named; wiring starts; the table
fills. Speak only in the pauses (or speed up in editing).
VO (over the first drops): "Now the bench builds itself from her question. Each apparatus lands as the question
names it."
[Let the app read "Connect the circuit as shown in the diagram" while the leads draw.]
VO (as rows fill): "The app does exactly what each sentence says: it connects the circuit, closes the key, and records
every reading as it's read. Then it plots V against I and finds the slope."

**1:40–2:10 · Practise and Exam**
[Screen] Home → **Practise**: drag one lead onto a terminal, take a reading. Then **Exam**: show the clock. Hand in →
Results with marks.
VO: "Then it's her turn. In Practise she wires the circuit herself, lead by lead, and takes her own readings.
In Exam it's the real thing: the clock runs, and when she hands in, she gets WAEC-style marks."

**2:10–2:35 · The examiner**
[Screen] Scroll to **Examiner's questions**. Hold the mic button and answer one question aloud; captions stream in;
the verdict and reply appear.
VO (after your spoken answer): "Then the examiner asks her about her work. She answers out loud. Meta Voice
Transcribe captions her as she speaks, and Muse Spark judges what she meant, even in Pidgin. It helps her learn. It
never changes her marks."

**2:35–2:50 · Under the hood**
[Screen] The GitHub README: the "How we use Meta's models" table, then the "Built with Muse Code" list.
VO: "One rule runs through everything: the AI reads and judges; the engine decides. The physics and the marks live in
code, so the same work always gets the same score. It's covered by over thirteen hundred tests, and Meta's Muse Code
built seventeen of its pull requests."

**2:50–3:00 · Close**
[Screen] Back to the home page and the URL.
VO: "SnapLab gives every student a lab in their pocket. Built during this hackathon, and live now at
snaplab-ng-2026.web.app."
